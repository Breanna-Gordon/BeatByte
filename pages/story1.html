Kelly Edwards: Composing for Indie Games

A composer’s perspective on balancing creative freedom, technical constraints and collaboration in independent game development

Kelly Edwards is an independent composer working primarily with small game studios and solo developers. For Kelly, indie games offer a unique creative space, where music can shape emotional tone without the constraints of large production pipelines.

"I started composing for games almost by accident" Kelly recalls. "A friend was developing a small narrative game and needed music. That first project taught me how different game composition is from traditional media. The music has to breathe with the gameplay and not just accompany it."

“Indie projects give you room to experiment” Kelly explains. “You’re often working closely with developers, responding directly to gameplay ideas and narrative changes in real time.”

However, this freedom comes with its own challenges. Limited budgets, evolving scopes and technical requirements mean that composers must think beyond traditional linear soundtracks.

“Writing for games isn’t just about music, it’s about understanding systems and how sound responds to player choice”

Composing Within Game Constraints

Kelly's workflow focuses on modular composition writing music that can loop seamlessly and adapt based on gameplay states. "I might create a main theme with three intensity layers" Kelly explains. "A calm exploration version, a tension puzzle solving layer and a high energy combat variation. Developers can transition between them based on what's happening in the game." This approach allows developers to implement audio without heavy technical overhead. Rather than delivering a single linear track, Kelly provides structured assets with clear loop points, stem separations and technical documentation. Tools that support adaptive layers and clear licensing help bridge the gap between creative intent and production reality. "The technical side used to be the hardest part" Kelly states. "Learning to export in formats game engines actually need, understanding sample rates and file compression, it's all part of the job now."

Finding the Right Platform

Kelly discovered BeatByte while searching for better ways to license work to multiple indie studios without renegotiating contracts each time. "I needed a system where licensing was built in from the start, not something I had to figure out project by project."

What drew Kelly to the platform was its focus on transparency and creator tools designed specifically for game audio. "Most music platforms don't understand adaptive layers or loop points. BeatByte does. I can upload a track with all its variations, tag it properly and developers can preview it directly in their engine."

Beyond the technical features, Kelly values the platform's approach to fair compensation. "The commission structure is clear and payments are reliable. It feels like the platform actually wants composers to succeed, not just extract value from us."

For Kelly, composing for indie games is as much about collaboration as it is about music. Platforms that respect creative labour and provide clear technical pathways make it easier for composers to focus on what matters most, creating meaningful player experiences.
